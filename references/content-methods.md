# Content methods learned from the repositories

## Research and voice

Identify the reader, the exact topic and the useful takeaway. For fresh developments,
read primary sources, retain URLs and dates, distinguish the announcement from its
implications, and say what remains unknown. A popular example can suggest a format;
it cannot establish why it performed well or prove a universal algorithm rule.

Keep style evidence separate from biographical evidence. Writing samples reveal
rhythm, vocabulary and formatting. A story bank holds user-confirmed projects,
results, dates, changes of mind and what may be named publicly. If an interview is
requested, follow the user's most concrete answer, clarify a vague claim once,
accept boundaries and record gaps rather than filling them. A focused interview
can produce a five-line post spine: situation, observation, evidence, lesson, next
step. Ordinary educational posts can proceed from documented facts without an
interview or a manufactured first-person narrative.

## Hooks and post structure

Use one main claim per post. Match a hook to evidence already available: a measured
result, a fair comparison, a concrete problem, a practical artifact, an explained
mechanism, a genuine turning point or an accurately attributed quotation. When
options help, give two or three structurally different hooks and choose one with a
brief reason. Make the first line intelligible alone, then deliver its promised
value early. Use short readable paragraphs and a substantive close when useful.

The source collections contain two separate libraries; their IDs are not interchangeable:

- Jake: [21 hooks](sources/Jakeschincariol/skills/li-post/hooks.json), including
  comparisons, mistakes, time savings, story openings and direct practical value.
- Sergey: [20 formulas](sources/sergebulaev/references/hook-formulas.md), including
  cost ledgers, historical evidence, controlled comparisons and evidence bridges.
- For founders specifically, Sergey also supplies
  [10 angles](sources/sergebulaev/references/founder-topics.md). Use founder, sales or investor framing only when it matches the user's audience.

These are optional structures, not verified performance rankings. Avoid adapting
sample first-person achievements as the user's own. A hook that overstates the
source needs rewriting even when it matches a template perfectly.

## Editing and review

Remove generic throat-clearing, repeated ideas, vague claims, performative
suspense, unrelated CTAs and accidentally pasted model/interface artifacts.
Replace inflated wording with concrete meaning. Preserve technical terms such as
robust statistics when they carry a precise meaning. Check whether the edit altered
uncertainty, causality, attribution or a number. Do not strip meaningful Unicode
characters from names, non-English text or emoji blindly.

Jake's humanizer is a mechanical lexicon plus five local style heuristics.
Sergey's newer humanizer uses vocabulary density, proportionate rhythm edits and
an over-correction check. Learn the editorial checks, not a claim that typography
or score thresholds can establish authorship. Do not add personal details, vary
sentences mechanically, or rewrite clean prose merely to satisfy a score.

For review-only requests, report concrete issues and proposed fixes without silently
rewriting. For rewrite requests, deliver the revised copy and note substantive
changes. Actual blockers include unsupported facts, wrong attribution, unresolved
placeholders, conflicts with the user's requirements and verified format limits;
source-author taste is not a universal blocker.

## Repurposing

Read the supplied asset before extracting claims, numbers, mechanisms, stories and
mistakes. Each proposed post should stand alone with its own useful angle. Preserve
source qualifications, attribution and facts. Remove irrelevant platform artifacts.
Do not squeeze a thin source into a fixed number of posts. Deliver the requested
number of finished drafts when requested, even if upstream prefers one at a time.

## Planning

Plan around the user's audience, evidence, available effort and goals. Record an
angle, evidence/source, format, hook direction and reader takeaway for each slot.
Rotate themes and formats to avoid repetition. Keep known recent drafts in mind.
A calendar is a plan, not a scheduled publishing job. Cadence and audience-local
times are testable assumptions unless account data supports them. Generic B2B
sales ratios and daily engagement quotas are unsuitable defaults for this user.

## Carousels and other visuals

A sequence, comparison or multi-part mechanism can justify multiple slides. A single
claim may work better as one image. Use an informative cover, one idea per slide,
readable type, a useful recap and a close appropriate to the goal. Adapt slide count
to content. Follow the applicable image, PDF or presentation skill when generating
an artifact; these repository notes do not supply a rendering tool.

When an existing application has a renderer, respect its configured backend.
Installing this skill does not add the optional upstream Pixfaro integration.
