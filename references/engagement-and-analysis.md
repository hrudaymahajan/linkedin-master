# Engagement drafts, profiles and measurement

## Comments and replies

Read the actual post and surrounding thread. If inaccessible, request the relevant
text rather than inferring it from a URL. Useful comment moves include answering
an open question, adding a sourced fact, identifying a missing condition, extending
a specific line, making a respectful correction, or sharing a confirmed experience.
Choose the move that contributes something; avoid generic praise and restatement.

Distinguish a top-level comment from a reply. For replies, retain author, target
comment and thread context. Answer substantive questions, acknowledge the true
part of disagreement and explain the remaining difference. Length should fit the
exchange, not a mandatory character quota. Do not invent lived experience.

For batches, separate substantive questions, prospective collaboration, peer
conversation, simple support and noise. Report counts and skipped categories.
Avoid duplicate replies and replies to the user's own comments. An old thread can
still deserve a public answer; the upstream 72-hour DM rule is a preference, not
a fact. Outputs are drafts for manual use in manual content mode.

## Inbox and outreach preparation

For supplied inbox messages, categorize possible leads, recruiters, peers, specific
asks and unwanted pitches. Treat classification as a judgment with evidence, not
proof that a message is automated. Draft responses where useful and do not archive,
delete or send anything as part of classifying it.

An outreach draft needs a real reason to contact that person, an accurate identity
and a clear, proportionate ask. Never invent a shared connection or pretend the
user read or used something they did not. Follow-ups should add value and respect
noninterest. Do not generate a recurring sender from a request for message copy.
Verify any account-specific note limit when it matters; the source collections
conflict on 200 versus 300 characters.

## Profiles

Start from sections actually provided, the user's goal and real evidence. Audit
headline, About, experience, featured work, visual presentation, relevant skills,
recommendations and contact clarity. Mark missing input as not assessed. Prioritize
changes by likely usefulness, then provide copy-ready rewrites. Job seekers need
portfolio and demonstrated work; business-development profiles may emphasize a
service and evidence. Do not force the latter framing onto everyone.

Jake supplies a [12-part, 100-point rubric](sources/Jakeschincariol/skills/li-profile/rubric.json);
Sergey supplies a [nine-section approach](sources/sergebulaev/skills/linkedin-profile-optimizer/SOURCE.md).
Scores are editorial rubrics, not LinkedIn scores or predicted conversion. Do not
penalize unseen sections or promise the repositories' claimed multipliers. A rewrite
cannot create recommendations, genuine achievements or a photo that does not exist.

## Analytics

Use the user's exports or supplied counts. Report the observation period, sample
size, definitions and missing fields. Useful calculations when denominators exist:

- Engagement rate = (reactions + comments + reposts) / impressions; multiply by 100
  when presenting a percentage.
- Comment-to-reaction ratio = comments / reactions.
- Reach multiple = impressions / follower count measured for the relevant period.
- Save or send rate = saves or sends / impressions, only when those fields exist.

Zero denominators or missing values are unavailable, not zero performance. Distinguish
mean post rate from weighted aggregate rate. Compare similar post ages, formats,
audience sizes and topics before suggesting a pattern. Small samples and association
cannot establish cause. Rank according to the user's goal; raw reach, conversation,
saves and business outcomes answer different questions.

For engager analysis, deduplicate known profile identifiers, retain source and
observation time, and match only supplied professional data to user-defined criteria.
Unknown role, company size or intent stays unknown. A reaction does not prove buying
intent. Anonymous viewers remain anonymous; do not attempt identity reconstruction.
Thread follow-up reports should say what was observed, not invent replies or urgency.

## Team advocacy

If requested, adapt an operating plan around voluntary participation, individual
expertise and voice, available time, company facts and confidentiality. Reusable
stages are voice capture, first drafts, a shared idea supply and a review rhythm.
Measure participation, content quality and clearly defined outcomes; summing members'
impressions does not establish unique team reach. Do not repeat identical posts
across employees or promise benchmark multipliers from the source repositories.
